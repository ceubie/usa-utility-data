Sewer Service Area Planning

DNR has approved updates to the Policies & Criteria for the Review of Sewer Service Area Amendments on April 8, 2026. New applicants for Sewer Service Area Amendments should begin referring to new documentation. Please refer to final documentation provided below.

Staff presented a detailed overview of the background and proposed changes at the December 11, 2025, Commission meeting. A Public Hearing was held at the January 8, 2026, Commission meeting and Commission action was taken at the March 12, 2026, Commission meeting (link to CARPC Resolution No. 2026-05). Refer to the Meeting Information page for more information and recordings of each meeting.
 
A summary of public commentary received and CARPC staff responses is available at this link. An overview of key changes is available at this link. 

Overview

Sewer Service Areas (SSA)—including Urban Service Areas (USA) and Limited Service Areas (LSA)—identify where sanitary sewer service already exists and adjacent lands most suitable for new development. Within these areas, Environmental Corridors are delineated identifying areas of environmentally sensitive features where development would have an adverse impact on water quality (see Environmental Corridors webpage for additional details).

SSA planning plays an important role in keeping Wisconsin’s water safe for drinking, recreation and diverse aquatic life. SSA planning is not intended to restrict a community’s growth, obligate wastewater treatment plants to provide sewer throughout the planning boundary, and/or affect community annexation policy. Planning accommodates future growth, while at the same time consolidating wetland, shoreland, and floodplain protection programs within a community-based plan for sewered development.

Effective service area planning and policies relating to wastewater collection and treatment are best developed in tandem with comprehensive planning at a regional and local level. The Capital Area Regional Planning Commission (CARPC) conducts water quality management planning, including SSA planning activities, under contract with Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources (DNR).

Sewer Service Area Amendments

Changes to SSA boundaries allow for a careful and structured approach to the expansion of urban areas to meet the needs of a growing region, while managing, protecting, and enhancing groundwater and surface water quality. Any change to SSA boundaries — including USA and LSA boundaries — requires an amendment to the Dane County Water Quality Plan (see Wisconsin DNR SSA Planning for additional info). These amendments are referred to as SSA amendments. This incremental planning approach considers the interconnected relationship and impact of land development on water resources and ecosystem resources on an areawide basis.

In general, the SSA amendment process includes a formal application, public hearing, review by CARPC staff, Commission action on a Water Quality Management Letter (WQML), and Wisconsin DNR final review and decision. The cost of CARPC staff time to administer SSA amendments is covered through direct billing to the applicant, as established through CARPC Resolution No. 2026-06 (link below). Mapping of all sewer service area amendments and associated documentation is available to view and download on our Open Data Portal.
