# Utility data inventory reassessment

Stopping-point review, November 9, 2026. This is a review of the collected files and documented source attempts, not proof that the collection includes every available source or every U.S. utility. See [README.md](README.md) for source descriptions and [GAP_ANALYSIS.md](GAP_ANALYSIS.md) for jurisdiction-specific gaps.

## What is present

A filesystem inventory made during this review found **7,882 files totaling approximately 11.79 GB** in `data/` (before this reassessment document was written). The total includes compressed archives, source metadata, download manifests and multiple snapshots; it is not a count of utilities or unique assets. There were 268 GIS `snapshot.json` directories, with 3,765,682 returned features summed across datasets. Every snapshot recorded equal start-of-collection and downloaded feature counts; this comparison does not establish real-world completeness, unique assets, boundary precision, or that the source stayed unchanged during paging. There are 52 top-level batch manifests, plus layer- and source-specific manifests. There are files in 37 state or territory directories; additional jurisdictions are represented in national datasets and GIS directories, so that directory count is not a geographic coverage count.

The strongest existing national coverage includes EIA electric utilities, plants, generators and reported county presence; EIA natural-gas companies through 2024 and selected pipeline layers; EPA drinking-water systems and mixed-source/modeled service areas; NPDES facilities and outfalls, CWNS and modeled/source sewersheds; public federal generation, transmission, dam and reclamation infrastructure; and FERC registrants and selected 2026 public filings. Historical FERC filings and PHMSA annual reports also exist as third-party preserved copies. These resources have different jurisdictions, reporting thresholds, reporting years, update cycles and identifiers. Many overlap each other, and none alone is a census of all utility providers or assets.

The newly collected Missouri PSC page contains 1,719 community/subarea table records, and a separate Missouri data-portal CSV contains 1,718 records but reports a 2017 last row update. North Dakota additions include a regulator provider list, an association reference map and directory, and the state 2025 grid plan with cooperative county/supplier information. The source URLs, dates, limitations and validation for these additions are in the README and `data/manifest_round25*.json`.

## Most consequential gaps

1. **Precise current service areas.** National EIA electric county reports indicate presence, not service everywhere in a county; national electric polygons and many state maps are approximate or older. A complete current national gas local-distribution territory dataset is still missing. Water and wastewater service-area files mix state-sourced boundaries and modeled or buffered estimates; some systems and territories are absent. Competitive suppliers, franchise holders, distribution operators and infrastructure owners are different roles. None of these data alone determines service at an individual property.
2. **Distribution and local infrastructure.** Most electric distribution circuits, local gas mains and connections, local water and sewer networks, district heating/cooling, propane and smaller private or tribal systems are not comprehensively mapped in this collection. Existing transmission, pipeline, dam and plant layers have different thresholds, statuses, coverage and vintages. Restricted or unpublished infrastructure cannot be made complete from these public files alone.
3. **Currency and provider universes.** The national gas company archive currently reaches 2024, and a number of state maps and inventories are several years older. FERC filings cover entities and forms within its jurisdiction, and recent submissions can be amendments for earlier reporting years. The Missouri portal CSV is explicitly historical. Update dates on a service or file do not prove every underlying record is current.
4. **Source discovery and reconciliation.** The state-by-state gap log identifies many opportunities for more regulator, municipal, tribal, cooperative and utility sources. More complete identification requires comparing entity names and IDs, source dates, provider roles, geographic coverage and overlaps. Such reconciliation has not been performed; the project preserves original sources.
5. **Access and incomplete attempts.** The New Mexico 2025 electric cooperative boundary item remains publicly discoverable, but its state map service returned an empty layer list and the attempted layer returned a JSON 404, so no geometry was saved. DOE/ORNL's onsite-energy application failed to render during the last attempt. The standalone FERC historical Form 714 CSV archive, some documentation and local source URLs returned HTTP 403; alternate archival FERC files exist but are not equivalent to a fresh direct download. Some state and local sources have explicit use restrictions, application errors, broken links or require a records request. See the source-specific entries in the gap log and manifests rather than assuming every attempt failed permanently or that every earlier failure remains unresolved.
6. **Validation limits.** Hashes, CSV row counts, ZIP CRC checks and GIS source-count comparisons detect many transfer errors; they do not establish factual accuracy, legal service rights, coordinate quality, currency, or complete coverage. A scan of recognizable list entries in top-level manifests found 43 failed attempts, some of which may have later alternatives. The original FERC archive progress manifest stopped early on a Windows file-replacement error; `data/manifest_round24_ferc_audit.json` independently records all 156 archive files as matched to publisher size and checksum.

## Priority order for a later continuation

- Obtain current authoritative state and utility electric and gas service boundaries where available, especially where the gap log identifies only coarse, old, or unavailable coverage.
- Add verified water and wastewater service boundaries and provider records from state and local authorities, preserving method, date and distinctions between actual, certificated, planned and modeled areas.
- Retry the New Mexico cooperative boundary and DOE/ORNL onsite-energy sources when their services work; pursue authorized alternatives for failed direct-source files, with particular attention to source vintage and usage terms.
- Expand provider-specific infrastructure coverage and underrepresented utility categories, while recording restrictions and comparing overlap with existing national layers.
- Build a separate source-inventory and currency review before any later harmonization; do not silently merge incompatible geographies, roles, dates or records.
